1

More than a list of skills

Goals connect learning to a real need—and keep that purpose visible across sessions.

Clarify the outcome

Help someone turn “be a better manager” into something useful to work on now. Use their context; don’t make them complete a planning exercise.

Choose the next move

Connect that outcome to relevant capabilities and a credible route. In the horizon vision, that could mean learning, practice, application, or assessment.

Keep the direction

Remember the purpose and useful work as the learner returns or changes course. Personal and assigned Goals keep their own ownership and requirements.

Not every question needs a durable Goal. The horizon PRDs prioritize a meaningful first action—not a completed Goal-setting form. Alpha implements a narrower static-path experience.

Grounded in the Skills Blueprint

The three Subskill names come from the Team Management example. Team Direction includes Goal and Measure Design, with the learning objective “Define measurable success criteria for each team goal.” Our Goal and focus are illustrative: learning that objective does not establish mastery of the whole Subskill.

The Blueprint defines the capability scope. The separate measurement brief proposes Foundational, Developing, Proficient, and Advanced. Those levels are not content difficulty, trust tiers, or validated self-report questions.

2

How it works today

Current Goal-setting design and local main-branch widget behavior. This is not a live-production audit.

  1. Understand the need

    A clear outcome can go straight to a proposal. For a broad or uncertain request, offer narrower directions or ask only useful questions. Clarification questions are optional.

  2. Propose one focused Goal

    Normally three to five central Subskills; fewer for a narrow need. Keep the learner’s ambition visible and explain what this first Goal covers.

  3. Let the learner confirm the scope

    The Goal widget shows the outcome and related skills. Learners can add or remove skills before confirming. At least one relevant Subskill must remain.

  4. Use context and get to learning

    “Use this goal” skips footing. Existing Wallet information can remove the need to ask again; partial information normally leaves only missing areas to answer.

Footing means the starting information used to help select content. An optional answer also lets the learner express where they think they stand. It is not a target or proof of proficiency.

Current widget patternGoal review · sample data

Reuses the Helix card, Badge, and Button components. Composition follows GoalReviewCard in local origin/main (5a53c72b47); catalog search and services are not connected.

What is missing: the current flow captures the destination as Goal text and Subskills, and the starting point as familiarity. It does not connect these to an agreed, measurable target proficiency. Current labels—Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced—also need a clear relationship to the Skills measurement model.
3

Proposed improvements for Alpha

Make the destination and starting point understandable, without adding gates before learning.

Connect the Goal to a desired capability

Offer an optional “What would you like to be able to do?” using concrete Subskill descriptions. For example: “Define clear goals and success measures reliably on a real task.”

This connects to target proficiency conceptually. In Alpha, it is a stated aspiration—not an assessed target, a guaranteed path outcome, or an admin requirement we can track.

Make footing understandable

Use the same capability descriptions when asking where the learner stands. Keep existing information’s source visible, and let the learner skip or say “I’m not sure.”

The proposed scale needs Skills review and learner testing. Understanding a level label cannot be assumed.

Illustrative · not a validated rubric

Team Direction: define clear team goals

FoundationalWith support and a worked example.
DevelopingUnaided, with deliberate effort.
ProficientReliably on a real task.
AdvancedIn unfamiliar situations or a new domain.

Example: “I want to do this reliably” describes the destination; “I can do it with support” describes the learner’s reported starting point. Neither is assessed evidence.

A proposal that needs explicit agreement. The supplied Alpha scope decision says not to set or track static-path target proficiency. Optional desired-capability wording explores that connection without a proficiency tracker. A saved target-level field would be a scope change—not something already agreed.

See the proposed Goal widget → Open “Describe where I want to get to” or “Review starting point.” Both are optional.

Keep the Expert follow-up optional

“I already know this” could mean “skip it” or “help me go deeper.” Ask which, without blocking the learner.

Skip: pass that preference to planning before acceptance. Deeper: clarify the desired outcome; current tagging does not guarantee advanced content. No answer: let the learner continue; our proposed default does not assume permission to exclude.

Wallet writes and planning ownership

The supplied PSW thread agrees that a submitted Expert answer creates a self-attested claim. That is separate from the optional learning preference and is not platform-attested or verified. Expert is not silently equated with Advanced.

The older thread assigns exclusion to LEX; the newer LEX PRD assigns construction to PSW / Planner and fixes the path after acceptance. Resolve this conflict before implementation. Required assigned learning is not waived by a learner’s self-report. If exclusions leave no useful learning, offer Goal refinement—not an empty path or an achieved Goal.

4

At the end: a useful reflection, not proof

Goal completion is not available in Alpha. The end-of-path Skill Check and the platform-attested proficiency claims it would produce are P1.

Specified LEX Alpha ending

“You’ve finished the learning in this path.”

Celebrate the selected items being complete. Show “Skills assessment coming soon.” No Goal-complete state, skill-progress bar, proficiency claim, or course certificate.

Additional Alpha proposal

“Where would you place yourself now?”

Offer the same optional Subskill questions again. If the learner described a desired capability, keep that aspiration visible—but never show “target achieved.”

With a comparable starting self-report, show the two answers. Without one, show the ending answer alone. Never add a mandatory starting survey just to get a comparison.

Example: wanted “reliably on a real task”; reported “with support” before; reports “reliably” now. We learned how the learner describes their ability—not that proficiency improved or the Goal was achieved.
What would make the signal useful?

Test whether learners understand the descriptions, whether the content felt appropriate for their starting point and aspiration, and whether their answers change. Report response and skip rates: optional answers are a selected sample, not an upskilling score.

Skills’ concern is valid: self-report alone is weak evidence for employers. A change may reflect confidence or a better understanding of the question. Do not turn it into verified growth, an admin capability ranking, or automatic Wallet updates. Only compare matching Subskill scope and the same question/scale; existing Wallet information is not a new baseline self-report.

What remains unproven in Alpha?

Self-report does not validate the assessment-to-proficiency loop. The LEX PRD says authored quiz completion produces no Platform-Observed skill signals in Alpha. Its absence is not fixed by collecting reflections.

The Expert write agreement does not settle ending-answer writes: agree those separately with Skills / PSW and preserve stronger evidence. Historical Platform-Observed data is also partial because only around 3K courses are tagged; missing history does not mean missing capability.

This prototype tests comprehension and usefulness. It does not validate live Goal mapping, path generation, or signal quality—the three hypotheses identified by Team Connect.

5

Keep the ambition. Right-size the next step.

Alpha

Narrow, don’t silently truncate

One focused Goal, up to five Subskills. If the request is bigger, agree a useful first outcome. Five is a planning limit—not the size of someone’s ambition.

Beta direction

Decompose a broader Goal

“Become an effective manager” could include monthly planning, developing the team, and leading change. Let the learner review connected smaller Goals and choose where to start. Keep the broader purpose visible.

Later direction

Change course without starting over

Allow Goal changes, show what changes in the path, and preserve useful work. Add assessed targets only when the coverage, thresholds, and evidence can support them.

A future skill-development Goal can be evaluated against agreed target capability and sufficient evidence. Even then, a partial assessment is not proof of a whole Subskill—and a broader work outcome may need more than a proficiency result.

Agree these before adding scope

Skills + PSW: plain-language level descriptions, whether desired-capability framing is appropriate for Alpha, and how reflection data is used—not automatically written to the Wallet.

PSW + Planner + LEX: optional Expert handling, exclusions before acceptance, and refinement when there is little left to teach.

Product + Design + LEX: whether optional reflection earns its place beyond the committed ending. Protect the direct route into learning.
