Goals in Helix

What do I want to be able to do? What do I need to learn? And what happens when my ambition is bigger—or changes?

A Goal is the outcome you want. Subskills describe the abilities it needs. A path is the learning chosen to help you get there.Interactive product discussion—not a PRD or a working product. Alpha focuses the learning; it does not measure skill progress or mark Goals complete.

1

A Goal gives the learning a purpose

“Learn Excel” names a topic. “Build a monthly spending report in Excel” tells us what the learner wants to do with it.

One example throughout

Build a monthly spending report in Excel

Sort and filter dataOrganize expenses by date and show only the month you need.
Calculate totals with formulasAdd up spending for the month and for each category.
Create chartsShow which categories account for the most spending.

Plain-language Subskill examples and illustrative outcomes, not official taxonomy labels or proficiency definitions.

Why it matters

For the learnerKnow why this learning is relevant and what it should help you do.
For the plannerChoose content for a specific outcome, rather than everything about Excel.
For judging success laterDefine what “able to do it” means. Finishing content alone cannot answer that.
Starting point / footing: “I’m a beginner at formulas.”
Target: “I need to calculate spending by category without help.”
Path: the learning that helps close that gap.

A target describes the capability needed for this Goal. Not everyone needs to become Advanced. Personal target-level collection still needs alignment; assigned targets come from the admin.

2

How big should a Goal be?

Small enough to explain what the learner will be able to do. A broad ambition can need several focused Goals—not an arbitrary five-Subskill slice presented as the whole thing.

Alpha · focused Goal

One manageable outcome, up to five Subskills

Start with something like the monthly spending report. If the request is broader, help the learner choose and confirm a smaller outcome before planning.

Five is the current planning boundary, not a definition of how big someone’s ambition can be. Do not quietly drop the rest or imply the smaller path covers it all.

Beta direction · broad Goal

Keep the ambition; break down the work

Help the learner review smaller, related Goals. Each gets its own Subskills, targets, and path, while the larger Goal explains how they fit together.

Consider expected effort, prerequisites, content availability, and what could be assessed—not only the number of Subskills. Splitting does not itself validate achievement.

Try a broader request

“I want to manage my small business finances in Excel.”

What the learner should see before accepting a split

The smaller outcomes, how each contributes to the larger Goal, their Subskills, suggested order, estimated effort where available, and assessment coverage or gaps. The learner can adjust the proposed split before accepting it. Do not invent estimates or imply the broad Goal is fully covered when it is not.

3

Alpha: use the starting point to plan

Footing has two jobs: help select appropriate content, and let the learner say what they already know and want to focus on. A level alone does not tell us what they want to leave out.

The personal Goal flow: describe the outcome → review the Subskills → share a starting level → confirm the Goal → get a path.
Explain upfront: “This learning helps you work toward your Goal. Goal achievement checking is not available in Alpha.”
Three different answers:
Starting level: “Where am I now?” → helps choose learning.
Target level: “Where do I want to get to?” → defines the intended Goal.
Ending self-report: “Where do I think I am now?” → supports reflection.
Target-setting for personal Goals is the intended direction; its Alpha scope still needs agreement. Assigned targets come from the admin.
Will learners understand the proficiency levels?

That is an open question, not something the shared labels solve. Skills has raised concerns about the reliability of self-claims and their usefulness to employers. Use plain-language descriptions of what a learner can do, aligned with Skills’ Subskill definitions, and test whether learners interpret them as intended.

For formulas, an example question to test is “Can you total spending by category on your own, with help, or not yet?” This is a wording exploration, not a new proficiency scale or an approved mapping. Use the same agreed wording at the beginning and end. Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced remain placeholders here. “I’m not sure” and skipping are not levels.

Assigned static path · Alpha

The admin already set the Goal and path

Keep the assigned Subskills, targets, and required content. We are not adding learner target-setting or pre-start path personalization here. A learner’s confidence does not waive the assignment or its requirements.

Switch to Personal Goal above to explore the proposed footing flow. End-of-path reflection is still shown below as an idea for discussion.

Try the proposal · sample answers prefilled

Where would you place yourself in each area today?

Choose the level that best describes what you can do. Your answers help us choose the learning for you; they do not prove your level or change your Goal’s targets. In the designed experience, each option needs the Skills-defined description for this Subskill.

Proposed place in the experience: draft Goal → starting-point answers → clarify any requested scope change → confirm the Goal → plan. Removing a Subskill changes the Goal’s scope; it never counts as achieving that Subskill.

Keep the detail

Pass each Subskill’s selected proficiency level, marked self-reported, and the learner’s intent to the planner. Preserve the level’s meaning. “Unsure” and no answer remain distinct. Keep any supplied target separate.

Respect selectable content

Select relevant items where supported. If only a whole Course is selectable, consider all the needed coverage before excluding it. Course-level tags are supporting metadata, not learner proficiency.

Keep uncertainty visible

“Advanced” does not automatically mean exclude or verified. “Check first” depends on assessment availability. If suitable content or a check is missing, explain that limitation.

4

Alpha: ask again, without claiming proof

Without an end-of-path assessment, ask learners to report the same Subskill proficiency again. Compare their answers—not measured ability. Any platform-attested evidence stays separate.

No Goal completion in Alpha. No result in this experience completes the Goal. There is no measured skill-progress view either. A learner can rate themselves again to see how their own view has changed—not to prove a proficiency increase.
Try the ending · all results are illustrative

This explores how to display a result if supplied. Finishing the path or passing an ordinary quiz does not automatically create platform attestation.

What we can learn in Alpha

Whether learners understand the questions, find the selected content appropriate, and report a different starting and ending level. This is useful feedback about the experience—not a reliable measure of how much they learned.

What this leaves unresolved

It gives admins no dependable proof that a learner can do the work. Repeating the question does not fix inaccurate self-ratings or prove the skills-development promise. Even the meaning of a level may become clearer after learning.

Proposed boundary: an answer here is not automatically a Wallet claim. Use it for planning and learner reflection. Whether it should become a persistent self-claim—or be shared with admins—needs a separate decision with Skills / PSW. Do not use it as employer-facing proof or overwrite platform-attested evidence.
Copy idea: “You’ve finished the learning in this path. Where would you place yourself in each area today? Compare your answers with where you started. These are your own ratings, not a measurement of skill progress. Goal achievement checking is not available in Alpha.”
The agreed end-of-path assessment placeholder is “coming soon.” It does not award attestation or complete the Goal.
What would Goal completion mean later?

Evidence that the learner meets the agreed target for every required Subskill—not simply finishing learning. Define what the evidence must cover before calling a Goal achieved. Platform-attested is an evidence category, not a proficiency level; a partial result cannot prove a whole Goal.

This is the future product definition, not a promise that Goal validation ships with Beta decomposition. Grouping smaller Goals and proving they were achieved are different pieces of work.

5

Later: let the Goal change with the learner

Changing the outcome is different from swapping a course. Show what changes, what stays, and what work carries over before asking the learner to confirm.

Future exploration · not Alpha
Preserve the learner’s work: review the revised outcome, Subskills, and targets; explain the effect on remaining learning; keep relevant completed work and history. Do not replace the current path until the learner confirms. Assigned Goals keep the admin’s permissions and requirements.

What still needs alignment?

The direction is clear. These details need agreement before the experience is committed.

1. Targets and future Goal achievement

Agree the target capability for each required Subskill and the evidence needed to establish it. Keep that definition consistent while Alpha exposes the validation gap. Product, Skills, Assessments, and Enterprise need the same meaning.

2. The LEX ending

Without an end-of-path assessment, ask for a second self-report using the same Subskills and proficiency descriptions. Show any platform-attested evidence separately, only when supplied by LEX / Skills. Neither completes the Goal in Alpha. The end-of-path assessment itself is a coming-soon placeholder.

3. Target proficiency and planning

Keep admin-provided targets. Do not add learner target-setting to assigned static paths. Agree whether personal Goal creation needs a clearer expected capability now, and what target proficiency collection belongs in follow-up work. Footing adjusts the route; it does not silently change the destination.

4. Understandable levels, useful signals, and the Wallet boundary

Skills supplies the Subskill proficiency definitions; Product and Design test whether learners understand them. Align before-and-after wording, allow uncertain or skipped answers, and do not assume ratings are comparable across learners. Decide separately whether these responses should become Wallet self-claims and who may see them. Their enterprise value is unproven.

5. What proves the end-to-end loop works?

Distinguish an Alpha experience that communicates the gap from a tested system that validates Goal targets. Reflection may be useful feedback, but it does not establish that assessment, proficiency calculation, and Goal achievement are connected.

6. Broad Goals and later edits

Confirm Beta decomposition scope and reconcile the WIP milestone labels. Define how a larger Goal preserves its meaning across smaller Goals, how coverage gaps are shown, and what users can change. Goal editing and validation belong in follow-up PRDs with their own scope.
