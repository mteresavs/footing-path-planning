How it works now—and the Alpha idea

Footing is optional. We should not create a questionnaire just so we can compare answers later.

Now · checked in local code

Use what we already know

“Use this goal” skips the familiarity form. When the Wallet has levels for all selected Subskills, “Edit skill levels” is hidden. With partial information, the form normally asks about missing areas.

Existing information is not a new learner self-report. The current familiarity labels are Not sure, Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced.

Alpha idea · for agreement

Offer review, without slowing down learning

Keep the direct route to learning. Let learners optionally review the starting information, answer missing questions, or clarify an Expert answer.

No required footing, follow-up, or target-level setup. Assigned paths retain the admin’s required learning; learner confidence does not waive it.

The horizon PRDs say to use existing context, avoid extensive setup, and prioritize a meaningful first action. A formal Goal is not required for every learning need; this example covers the learner who wants a path.

A Goal gives the learning a purpose

“Create a clear plan for my team’s next month.”

Team DirectionFor this Goal: set team goals and define success.
Work CoordinationFor this Goal: organize the work.
Team CollaborationFor this Goal: agree how the team will work together.

Actual Subskill names from the Skills Blueprint’s Team Management example. The Goal and focus statements are illustrative—not full coverage of those Subskills.

Why use the Blueprint?

It gives us a credible capability scope. Team Management → Team Direction → Goal and Measure Design → “Define measurable success criteria for each team goal.” That objective is only part of the Subskill. Completing related content cannot establish mastery of the whole area.

The Blueprint does not make course difficulty, footing, proficiency, and trust tiers interchangeable. The measurement brief proposes Foundational, Developing, Proficient, and Advanced; learner-friendly questions and mappings still need Skills agreement. The companion illustrates those descriptions, not a shipped scale. “Expert” is modeled separately as the explicit self-claim from the supplied thread—not silently equated with Advanced.

The earlier thread says “LEX handles exclusion.” The supplied LEX PRD instead assigns personal-path construction to PSW / Planner and fixes the content once accepted or assigned. Reconcile those statements. This proposal places exclusions before acceptance; LEX must not remove or reorder content based on footing or results during learning. Author-changed or removed content is a separate exception in the LEX PRD.

4

Alpha ending: reflect, don’t certify

The static-path Skill Check and its platform-attested proficiency claims are P1. A self-report does not replace that missing assessment.

Committed ending + an optional idea

The LEX Alpha PRD specifies a celebration for finishing all selected items and “skills assessment coming soon.” No course certificate or Goal completion.

Our additional proposal: “Where would you place yourself in these areas now?” Use the same agreed Subskill descriptions; offer “I’m not sure” and Skip. This reflection is not committed LEX P0 scope.

What the answers mean

Matching starting self-report? Show the two answers as self-reports. No matching answer? Show the ending answer on its own.

A different answer may reflect confidence or a changed understanding of the question—not measured skill growth. Do not compare an existing assessment or an old, different scale as though it were the learner’s starting answer.

Why consider it? It could help learners reflect and help us understand the Alpha experience. It cannot prove upskilling, Goal achievement, or the skills-validation loop. Keep it lightweight and optional; test whether it is useful.
What can Alpha—and this prototype—actually test?

Team Connect separates three hypotheses: Goal-to-skill mapping, coherent skills-aligned path generation, and reliable skill signals. A mock prototype can test whether the experience and proposed signals are useful; it does not validate any live mapping, generation, or signal quality.

A narrower live path can test usage of learning and authored quizzes, but does not prove proficiency. An optional self-report adds reported experience, not reliable assessment evidence. The release-options document presents choices, not a decision; the supplied LEX PRD gives the concrete P0 boundary we use here.

Wallet and admin boundaries

The Expert write agreement does not settle how every ending answer should update the Wallet. Confirm that separately with PSW / Skills. Do not silently overwrite stronger or older evidence.

The supplied Alpha scope discussion frames activity as “Skill in development” with consumption metrics—not proficiency strength. Preserve the metric’s meaning: learning activity is not measured skill progress. The LEX PRD explicitly says Alpha quiz completion produces no Platform-Observed skill signals; do not manufacture a Wallet signal from that completion.

Around 3K tagged courses means historical Platform-Observed coverage and workforce answers are partial; absent history is not proof of absent capability.

Keep the ambition. Right-size the next step.

Alpha

One focused outcome, up to five Subskills

Help narrow a broad request; don’t quietly drop the rest and claim to cover it. This is a planning boundary, not the size of someone’s ambition.

Beta direction

Related smaller Goals for a broad ambition

“Become an effective team manager” could become a monthly planning Goal, a team development Goal, and a handling-change Goal. Let the learner review the split; keep the larger ambition visible.

Later PRDs: change a Goal without losing useful work; define and validate target proficiency when the assessment loop can support it; support genuinely deeper learning. These are not extra Alpha setup steps.

What needs agreement

Three decisions—not a new required flow

1. PSW + Skills: understandable self-report wording, the Expert write, and whether ending answers should create any additional claims.

2. Planner + PSW + LEX: reconcile exclusion ownership, agree the skipped-follow-up policy, and decide what to do when little learning remains.

3. Product + Design + LEX: whether an optional ending reflection is worth adding beyond the committed completion moment and assessment placeholder. Protect the direct path into learning.
