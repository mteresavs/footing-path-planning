Goals, footing, and what “done” means

What are we helping the learner achieve? How should their starting point shape the path? And what can we honestly say when they reach the end in Alpha?

The Goal is the outcome. The path is a way to work toward it. Evidence tells us what we can say they achieved.Proposed product direction and Alpha experience, for alignment. The interactions below use example data; they do not run a planner or an assessment.

What is a Goal?

A meaningful outcome the learner wants—or is assigned—to achieve, expressed through the relevant Subskills and what they need to be able to do.

One example throughout

Build a monthly spending report in Excel

Sort and filter dataOrganize expenses by date and show only the month you need.
Calculate totals with formulasAdd up spending for the month and for each category.
Create chartsShow which categories account for the most spending.

Plain-language Subskill examples and illustrative outcomes, not official taxonomy labels or proficiency definitions.

The destination needs to be clear

The intended model is a Goal with target proficiency for its required Subskills. The target says what “enough” means for this particular outcome.

Starting point: “I’m a beginner at Excel formulas.”
Destination: “I need to calculate monthly spending by category without help.”
Evidence: “In a practical check, I correctly calculated the totals from a new set of expenses.”

These are three different pieces of information. The planner should not turn a starting-point answer into a target or assume every learner must become Advanced.

Where Alpha stands, from the discussion: personal Goal setting captures the outcome, up to five Subskills, and familiarity. A measured target-proficiency completion loop is not established. Admin targets, where supplied, remain inputs even when we cannot yet validate them.
2

What would Goal completion mean?

Proposed definition: the learner meets the agreed targets for the required Subskills, supported by evidence appropriate to those targets.

  1. Define the expected capability

    What should the learner be able to do, and at what level, for each required Subskill?

  2. Know what the assessment covers

    Match its result to the relevant outcomes and target level. An assessment may cover only part of one Subskill.

  3. Check the whole agreed Goal

    Confirm achievement only when the evidence meets the criteria for all required targets. Missing coverage stays visible.

A high score on an introductory assessment does not, by itself, mean Advanced proficiency. Passing one assessment does not prove the entire Goal.

Evidence type and proficiency level answer different questions

Self-reportedWhat the learner says about their ability or confidence.
Platform-observedActivity we can see, such as starting or completing learning. This can support “started learning” or “in progress.”
Platform-attested / verifiedStronger claims governed by the Skills and Assessments teams. The evidence must still cover the Goal’s targets.

These are not Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced levels. Assessment results still have value before full Goal validation exists: they show performance on the parts assessed and can guide what to work on next.

What is footing, and how do we use it?

The learner’s own description of their starting point, per Subskill. Use it to select and order relevant learning toward the Goal.

The admin already set the Goal and path

Keep the assigned Subskills, targets, and required content. We are not adding learner target-setting or pre-start path personalization here. A learner’s confidence does not waive the assignment or its requirements.

How familiar are you with each area?

Your answers help us choose where to begin and what to spend more time on. They do not change the Goal’s targets or prove proficiency.

Proposed place in the experience: draft Goal → starting-point answers → clarify any requested scope change → confirm the Goal → plan. Removing a Subskill changes the Goal’s scope; it never counts as achieving that Subskill.

Keep the detail

Pass each Subskill’s answer, its source, and the learner’s intent to the planner. “Unsure” and no answer remain distinct. Keep any supplied target separate.

Respect selectable content

Select relevant items where supported. If only a whole Course is selectable, consider all the needed coverage before excluding it. Course-level tags are supporting metadata, not learner proficiency.

Keep uncertainty visible

“Advanced” does not automatically mean exclude or verified. “Check first” depends on assessment availability. If suitable content or a check is missing, explain that limitation.

What does the ending mean in Alpha?

Proposed Alpha experience: acknowledge finishing the path, invite a reflection, and show any assessment evidence with its actual coverage. Goal achievement stays unconfirmed.

This is the gap to make explicit: Alpha does not yet establish that the learner reached the Goal’s target proficiency. We should not create a “Goal complete” result from consumption, confidence, or an unmapped pass score.
Try the ending · all results are illustrative

For the learner

Celebrate the learning completed. Compare the same optional familiarity question before and after. Allow unchanged, lower, unsure, or skipped answers. Show these as self-reports.

For the admin

Report completed activity, available assessment results, and whether the target has been validated separately. A confidence answer cannot satisfy a proficiency or compliance requirement.

For Profile / Wallet

Preserve the source and scope of every signal. Reflecting on familiarity does not award attested or verified proficiency. Whether reflection becomes a Wallet self-claim needs agreement.

What do we need to agree on?

The product choices come first. These are the conversations needed to turn this proposal into an experience.

1. One definition of Goal achievement

Agree the target capability for each required Subskill and the evidence needed to establish it. Keep that definition consistent while Alpha exposes the validation gap. Product, Skills, Assessments, and Enterprise need the same meaning.

2. The minimum Alpha ending

Agree “Path finished” plus optional reflection, with Goal achievement unconfirmed. Decide where each appears for learners and admins, and what happens when assessments are unavailable, partial, or not passed. No invented skill-progress percentage.

3. Target proficiency and planning

Keep admin-provided targets. Do not add learner target-setting to assigned static paths. Agree whether personal Goal creation needs a clearer expected capability now, and what target proficiency collection belongs in follow-up work. Footing adjusts the route; it does not silently change the destination.

4. Self-reports and Wallet writes

Align with Skills / PSW on the meaning, wording, and storage of beginning and ending self-reports. A reflection, an explicit self-claim, and assessment evidence need distinguishable sources. The examples here do not settle a Wallet write policy.

5. What proves the end-to-end loop works?

Distinguish an Alpha experience that communicates the gap from a tested system that validates Goal targets. Reflection may be useful feedback, but it does not establish that assessment, proficiency calculation, and Goal achievement are connected.
