What is a Goal?

A Goal describes what a learner wants or needs to be able to do. Helix turns that intent into focused learning and, ultimately, evidence of the relevant skills. The Goal is the outcome; the Path is the plan to work toward it.

For example, “Create a clear plan for my team’s next month” brings together Team Direction, Work Coordination, and Team Collaboration from the Team Management blueprint.

Anatomy of a Goal

Outcome
The capability to develop, with context about why it matters.
Subskills
The capabilities supporting the outcome. Up to five in Alpha; fewer when enough.
Target / due date
When the outcome is needed. Optional for learner-created Goals; an assigned Goal may have an admin deadline. This is not a content-duration budget.
Source & requirements
Learner-created or assigned, including requirements the learner cannot waive.
Target proficiency
The level needed for each required Subskill—not one level for the whole Goal. Future assessed model; not set or tracked in static Alpha.
Goal completion means demonstrating the required capability.Assessment evidence must support the target proficiency for the required Subskills. Finishing content or reaching a due date is not enough.

In Alpha: show content completion and “Skill in development”—not measured proficiency progress or Goal completion. Admin reporting is consumption-only while skills assessments are unavailable.

Proficiency and evidence

Foundational, Developing, Proficient, and Advanced describe proficiency. Platform-attested and platform-verified describe the evidence behind a claim. A check covering only part of a Subskill cannot prove proficiency across the whole Subskill; Skills and Assessments must define sufficient coverage.

2

How a Goal becomes a learning Path

  1. Narrow the intent

    Turn “Become a better manager” into a useful first outcome, such as planning the team’s next month. Ask only what is needed to get started.

  2. Confirm the scope

    Review the outcome, Subskills, and any target date in the Goal widget.

  3. Understand the starting point

    For learner-created Goals, reuse available context and offer optional footing where useful. Skip learner footing for assigned Goals in Alpha: their Paths are not personalized.

  4. Generate, review, and start

    The planner selects learning for the confirmed scope and context. The learner reviews the Path before starting; for assigned static Paths, the admin approves the content.

Planner handoff: outcome and context, confirmed Subskills, optional date, sourced footing, and any stated learning intent. Include supported constraints such as language or maximum learning time. A date and a time budget are different inputs.

Exact input contracts and content-depth support still need confirmation with the planner team.

3

How footing works

Current familiarity, used to choose relevant learning.

Footing checks how familiar the learner is with each Subskill. It helps identify where foundations are useful and where the learner wants more depth. It does not create a proficiency claim in the Wallet.

Footing is optional for self-initiated Goals. It helps tailor content, but learners should not have to rate every Subskill before they can start learning. Use available context when they skip; an unanswered question means unknown, not beginner. Assigned Goals skip footing entirely in Alpha because their Paths are not personalized.

Selecting Advanced opens an optional question about intent. It does not assume the learner wants to skip the Subskill.

Design still iterating · Advanced follow-up not yet implemented in main. Try the sliders below; changes stay in this example.

What each choice means for planning

Remove from this Goal
Narrow the Goal before planning and pass only the remaining Subskills. This does not delete a Wallet skill or prove it is known.
Teach me advanced applications
Keep the Subskill and pass a preference for more depth. Suitable content depends on tagging and coverage.

This scope change applies to learner-created Goals, not permission to waive assigned requirements. If nothing remains to learn, help the learner refine the Goal.

How does each Path use this information?

The confirmed Goal defines what to learn. Footing and intent help determine where to start; how they affect the learning depends on the Path.

Static Path

Footing guides content search.

How it is used

The planner uses Subskill familiarity and intent in semantic search for authored content, before the learner accepts the Path.

Advanced familiarity

If the learner wants more depth, seek applied material rather than foundational introductions. Keep the Subskill unless the learner chooses to remove it from the Goal.

Some experience, starting fresh, or unknown

Use the available context to find a useful starting point. There is no fixed rule to skip particular lesson types; unanswered footing stays unknown.

What changes—and what does not

Footing affects content selection, not a verified proficiency match or guaranteed removal of introductory items within selected sections. The accepted Path stays fixed.

Assessment

Static Alpha has no end-of-path skills assessment. Familiarity does not prove the Goal is complete.

Alpha boundary: self-initiated footing is optional. Assigned Goals skip it; everyone receives the same admin-approved Path.

Adaptive Path

Footing adjusts where teaching starts.

How it is used

LEX uses each Subskill’s footing to include or skip teaching bundles, and explains what was left out.

Advanced familiarity

The adaptive draft calls this “Expert”: skip that Subskill’s teaching bundles, while keeping it in the Goal and its assessment requirements.

Some experience, starting fresh, or unknown

Intermediate: keep the bundles, skip “Why” and “What,” and begin at “How.” Novice or no data: offer the full experience; unknown is not a novice claim.

What changes—and what does not

Familiar basics can be omitted without narrowing the Goal. An experienced PM learning AI for PRDs may skip PRD basics while retaining AI applications in that context.

Assessment

Expert in every required Subskill can lead straight to the final assessment, where available—not automatic Goal completion.

Alignment remaining: confirm the Expert/Advanced mapping and how “teach me advanced applications” affects exclusion. These are aligned draft rules; shipping status is unverified. Add-back is P2; remediation after a failed assessment needs separate scope.

How Goals expand in Beta

Future direction; delivery depends on the capabilities below.

Connected Goals and Paths for broad ambitions

Keep “Become a better manager” as the broader ambition. Break it into focused Goals—plan the team’s work, develop team members, and lead a change—each with its own Subskills and Path. Recommend an order, let the learner choose where to start, and review each Goal just before starting its Path.

Broad Goal Decomposition · PR #5211 · Read the PRD

Changes after learning starts

Let learners revise the Goal and add, remove, or change Subskills. Preview the Path changes before confirmation, preserve relevant completed work, and respect assigned requirements.

Assessed completion and admin signals

Helix recommends target proficiency for each Subskill based on the Goal and available learner knowledge, with an explanation of what the target means. When assessment evidence supports all required targets, mark the Goal complete. For assigned Goals, send supported completion and proficiency signals to admins with their evidence and trust tier.

Requires agreed targets, assessment coverage, and reporting permissions.
